This research aims to explore the performance of Averaged Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD) under varying network widths, particularly focusing on its generalization capabilities within the Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK) framework. The hypothesis posits that by investigating the behavior of averaged SGD in finite-width conditions, we can uncover deeper insights into generalization mechanisms. To achieve this, we will establish benchmarks comparing averaged SGD with standard SGD across different network architectures. Additionally, we will analyze how varying network widths influence generalization performance. An ablation test will involve evaluating averaged SGD on adversarial examples to assess its robustness under challenging conditions. We expect our findings to reveal critical insights into the relationship between network width and generalization, aligning with recent advancements in the field. However, we will ensure that our claims remain calibrated, avoiding overgeneralization beyond the tested scenarios.
