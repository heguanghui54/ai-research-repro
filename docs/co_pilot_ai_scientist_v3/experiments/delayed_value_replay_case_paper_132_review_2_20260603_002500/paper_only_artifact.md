This study investigates the convergence rates of Averaged Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD) within the Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK) framework, emphasizing the interplay between network width and generalization performance. The hypothesis posits that while averaged SGD can achieve optimal rates under NTK assumptions, its generalization ability may be sensitive to the width of the neural network. To explore this, we will derive theoretical bounds for averaged SGD and conduct numerical experiments that vary the width of the networks. A key component of our approach will be an ablation test, where we will evaluate the performance of averaged SGD using non-smooth activation functions, thereby stress-testing the robustness of our findings against the original assumptions. We expect our results to reveal a nuanced relationship between network width and generalization, contributing to the understanding of averaged SGD in practical settings. However, we will be cautious not to overclaim the generalization capabilities of our findings, ensuring that our conclusions remain grounded in the theoretical framework established.
