This follow-up study builds upon the findings of E2ENet, focusing on the Dynamic Sparse Feature Fusion (DSFF) mechanism's role in enhancing 3D medical image segmentation. The hypothesis asserts that DSFF can improve both efficiency and accuracy in segmentation tasks. The methodology involves developing a model that incorporates DSFF and evaluating its performance against traditional segmentation methods. The experiment will utilize a medical imaging dataset, emphasizing metrics related to efficiency and accuracy. An ablation study will be conducted to isolate the contributions of the DSFF mechanism. Expected outcomes include improved segmentation accuracy with reduced computational demands. However, potential failure modes include overfitting and neglecting deployment constraints. The study will refrain from claiming that DSFF is the sole solution for segmentation efficiency, instead focusing on its demonstrated benefits.
