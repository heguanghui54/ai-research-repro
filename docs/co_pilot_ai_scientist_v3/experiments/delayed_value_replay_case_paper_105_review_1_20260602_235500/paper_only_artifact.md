This study proposes a novel approach to 3D medical image segmentation through Dynamic Sparse Feature Fusion (DSFF). The hypothesis posits that DSFF can significantly enhance segmentation efficiency while maintaining accuracy, particularly under deployment constraints. The methodology involves developing a compact segmentation model that integrates DSFF, with a focus on balancing computational efficiency and accuracy. The experimental plan includes testing the model on a widely-used medical imaging dataset, comparing its performance against established architectures such as U-Net and newer transformer-based models. An ablation study will be conducted to isolate the effects of the DSFF mechanism on segmentation outcomes. Expected results include improved accuracy and reduced computational demands. However, potential failure modes may arise from overfitting and insufficient consideration of deployment scenarios. The study will not claim universal superiority over existing methods but will aim to provide a practical solution for efficient segmentation in clinical settings.
