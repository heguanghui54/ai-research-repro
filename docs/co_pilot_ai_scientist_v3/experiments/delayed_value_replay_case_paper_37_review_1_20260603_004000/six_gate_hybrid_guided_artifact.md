This research explores the intersection of human insights and automated causal inference methods, positing that human feedback can refine automated approaches to enhance treatment effect generalization. The hypothesis suggests that integrating human insights can improve the accuracy of treatment effect estimation across varying designs. To test this, we will conduct experiments that incorporate human feedback into the automated causal inference framework, evaluating its impact on treatment effect estimation under different covariate distributions. An ablation test will vary the amount of human feedback provided to assess its influence on model performance. We expect that the model will demonstrate improved accuracy in treatment effect estimation when human insights are integrated. However, potential failure modes include confusing prediction generalization with causal identification and over-reliance on human feedback without empirical validation. Importantly, this study does not claim that human insights can replace automated methods entirely, allowing for a balanced approach to causal inference.
