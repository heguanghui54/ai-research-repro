This study investigates a novel approach to causal inference, focusing on the ability to accurately infer treatment effects in the presence of covariate shifts. The hypothesis suggests that the proposed method can effectively estimate treatment effects, even when the underlying distribution of covariates changes. To validate this hypothesis, we will create a dataset featuring binary treatments and varying covariates. The model will be trained on one design and tested on another to evaluate its performance in estimating treatment effects. An ablation test will be conducted by omitting specific covariates to assess the robustness of the model. We anticipate that the model will yield reliable treatment effect estimates across different designs. However, potential failure modes include confusing prediction generalization with causal identification and neglecting treatment-effect metrics. Importantly, this research does not claim that the proposed method is universally applicable to all causal inference scenarios.
