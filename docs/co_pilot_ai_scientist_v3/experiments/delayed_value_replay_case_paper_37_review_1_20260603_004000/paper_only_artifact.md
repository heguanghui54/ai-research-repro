This research aims to explore the efficacy of weighted representations in causal inference across different designs. The hypothesis posits that these representations can generalize treatment effects effectively, even when faced with covariate shifts. To test this, we will create a synthetic dataset that simulates varying treatment policies and covariate distributions. The experiment will involve a train/test design shift, where the training data is generated under one design and the testing data under another. We will measure the treatment effects using weighted representations and expect to find that the model retains accuracy despite changes in the covariate distribution. An ablation test will be conducted by removing specific covariates to evaluate the robustness of the treatment effect estimation. Potential failure modes include confusing correlation with causation and neglecting unobserved confounders. Importantly, this study does not claim that weighted representations are the sole method for causal inference, allowing for a nuanced understanding of their applicability.
