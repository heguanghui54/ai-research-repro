## Interpretation

The package now satisfies the requested artifact pipeline: English paper,
focused English conference-style paper, usage guides, a reusable Codex
skill, reproducibility artifacts, and a pushed GitHub branch. It also has a
distinctive method name and mechanism:
Insight-Gated Research Evolution (IGRE), with scientific taste/insight logged
as a high-variance search prior rather than as preference labeling or generic
approval.

The evidence bar for the original target is still higher than the current
package. The strongest current claim is:

> IGRE is a reproducible co-pilot architecture and pilot evidence package for
> inserting human scientific taste, evaluator stress tests, frontier steering,
> verifiable micro-evolution, and claim calibration into AI Scientist-v2-style
> research loops.

The current package should not yet claim:

- human gates improve average benchmark performance;
- human gates improve paper quality;
- the full co-pilot system outperforms autonomous AI Scientist-v2;
- human attention efficiency has been measured;
- the paper is ready for a strong ML/NLP systems conference without further
  experiments.

The next evidence milestone is to scale beyond the single-author Codex trace
and the current negative short-budget FML evidence. The equal-context
OpenReview ablation has now addressed the easiest context-confound critique;
the gate-structure, gate-outcome, and single-gate artifact ablations now address
the easier "these are just borrowed labels" critique. The high-tail
power-analysis artifact now addresses the critique that breakthrough-probability
claims were unfalsifiable: such claims require large matched samples, with
roughly 500 runs per arm for a 5% to 10% tail-rate lift under the current
design assumptions. A blind-review packet is
prepared for the paired mini-manuscripts. The packet now includes a
preregistered analysis plan that defines useful taste/insight signals as
comments capable of changing research-control decisions rather than generic
approval. The sharper remaining priority is recruiting qualified human expert
raters, collecting blind scores, reporting inter-rater agreement,
useful-signal themes, and then expanding matched autonomous versus human-gated
runs across more tasks, seeds, and budgets. The delayed-value candidate-mining
screen now provides a filter: it finds 120 candidate comments from 473 reviews
that combine long-horizon directionality with short-term friction and should be
prioritized for expensive TFR replay. A small OpenAlex validation adds weak
screening support: after a title-overlap match-drift guard, delayed candidates
score 0.24 against citation-frontier terms versus 0.176 for controls. This
strengthens the workflow design but does not change the evidence boundary. A
second priority is scaling the
retrospective frontier-alignment protocol with independent semantic and human
future-frontier matching: the expanded citation probe, direct review-snippet
probe, and model-judged semantic probe still find `0` delayed-value cases,
which means candidate comments must be replayed and validated before being used
as a taste oracle. The
paper's
strongest practical target should remain human-participation workflow design:
identify which gates, feedback formats, and taste-prior mechanisms best
translate human insight into useful search pressure. The FML packages prove the
evidence shape can be generated on an AI Scientist-v2-style benchmark; they
currently argue against short-budget average-performance superiority and do not
prove paper-quality gains, attention efficiency, or superiority over autonomous
AI Scientist-v2.
