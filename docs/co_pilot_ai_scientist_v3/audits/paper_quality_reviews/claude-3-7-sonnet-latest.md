## 3. Top Three Strengths

**S1 – Honest self-assessment of negative results.** The paper is unusually candid: it explicitly reports that every FML-bench prospective package favors the autonomous baseline, that the online smoke continuation is worse than the autonomous run, and that the two model-reviewer recommendations disagree. This intellectual honesty is commendable and rare in systems papers.

**S2 – Conceptually motivated gate taxonomy.** The five-gate IGRE decomposition (taste prior, evaluator stress test, frontier steering, micro-evolution, claim calibration) is a coherent and principled attempt to operationalize "scientific taste" as a distinct concept from preference labeling or oversight. The separation of taste/preference/oversight in §2.1 is the paper's clearest theoretical contribution.

**S3 – Structured audit trail and schema design.** The gate-log schema, attention-cost fields, taste/insight fields, and the prospective matched-budget package validator represent genuine engineering work toward reproducible human-in-the-loop research. The release-readiness audit and clean-clone reproducibility check are good practices that many systems papers skip.

## 4. Top Five Blocking Weaknesses

**W1 – No credible empirical support for any central claim.** The paper's core hypothesis—that human gates improve research quality—is not supported by any experiment in the submission. Every FML-bench prospective package is negative for the co-pilot path (autonomous wins all three valid comparisons). The single positive result is a controlled Max-Cut micro-task that does not involve AI Scientist-v2, paper generation, or any meaningful notion of "scientific quality." The paper acknowledges this throughout, but the result is that the submission has no positive empirical finding tied to its central claim. A systems venue requires at least one controlled, statistically interpretable result that supports the proposed method.

**W2 – Circular and self-referential evaluation.** Nearly all "evidence" is generated by the same model family (Monica-routed GPT-4o-mini or Claude) that is also used to build the system. The paper-quality scores, participation-mode rankings, structured-feedback comparisons, OpenReview regeneration wins, and hypothesis-frontier rankings are all produced by the same or closely related models evaluating artifacts they helped generate. The cross-model Claude review reduces this bias slightly but still gives only 3/6 wins for the review-guided condition. No independent human expert evaluation exists anywhere in the submission. This is a fatal validity problem for a paper claiming to improve scientific quality.

**W3 – Structural incoherence: the paper is a lab notebook, not a research paper.** The manuscript reads as a sequential log of every experiment attempted, including failed network connections, slow dataset downloads, smoke tests, and measurement-readiness audits. Section 3 alone contains ~4,000 words of incremental experiment narration. There is no clean separation of method, experiments, and results. A top-venue paper requires a crisp problem statement, a method section that stands alone, and an experiments section with a clear hypothesis-per-experiment structure. The current document cannot be revised into that shape without a near-complete rewrite.

**W4 – The "high-tail hypothesis" is unfalsifiable as currently framed.** The paper repeatedly retreats to the claim that human gates may improve "rare, high-novelty, high-impact outcomes" even when average performance is worse. This is a legitimate scientific hypothesis, but the paper provides no operationalization of "high-tail outcome," no statistical framework for testing it (e.g., extreme-value theory, tail probability estimation), no sample size analysis for how many runs would be needed to detect such an effect, and no pilot data suggesting the tail distribution differs between conditions. As written, this framing functions as an unfalsifiable escape hatch rather than a testable scientific claim.

**W5 – Single-author, single-institution, single-task-family evidence with no external validity.** All human gate decisions are made by the paper's sole author. The "Human Co-Pilot Trace Dataset" is explicitly a single-author longitudinal corpus. The FML-bench experiments use one task family (Causality_causalml) with two-step budgets. The OpenEvolve results use toy combinatorial problems. There is no evidence that the architecture generalizes to other researchers, other domains, or other research workflows. For a paper claiming to design "collaborative automated science," the absence of any collaborator or external user is a fundamental validity gap.

## 5. Concrete Required Revisions

**R1 – Restructure the document into standard paper format before resubmission.** The paper must have: Abstract (≤250 words), Introduction (problem + contributions), Related Work, Method (IGRE gates described once, cleanly), Experiments (one subsection per hypothesis, with setup/results/analysis), Limitations, and Conclusion. All incremental narration, smoke-test logs, measurement-readiness audits, and "we then ran…" sequences must be moved to appendices or supplementary material. The main body should be ≤10 pages.

**R2 – Run a statistically powered matched-budget experiment before resubmission.** The minimum credible experiment is: ≥10 matched pairs (co-pilot vs. autonomous) across ≥3 distinct tasks, with a pre-registered primary metric, a two-sided paired test, and a power analysis justifying the sample size. If the result remains negative, the paper should reframe as a negative result paper with a clear explanation of why human gates fail at short budgets—that is a publishable contribution. If positive, report effect size and confidence intervals.

**R3 – Replace model-self-evaluation with at least one form of independent assessment.** Options include: (a) recruiting 3–5 domain experts to blind-review matched manuscript pairs, (b) using a held-out rubric derived from OpenReview data that was not used during system design, or (c) submitting generated papers to an actual automated benchmark with a fixed external evaluator (e.g., PaperBench). The current model-evaluates-model loop is not acceptable as primary evidence.

**R4 – Operationalize the high-tail hypothesis or remove it.** Either (a) define a concrete operationalization of "high-tail outcome" (e.g., top-k% on a multi-dimensional rubric, acceptance at a target venue, citation impact proxy), specify a statistical test, and run enough trials to have power to detect the effect, or (b) remove the high-tail framing from the abstract and conclusion and reframe the paper as a system design and feasibility study. The current framing promises more than the paper can deliver.

**R5 – Add at least two human participants beyond the author.** Even a small user study (3–5 researchers using the IGRE workflow on a real task) would provide external validity for the gate design and attention-cost measurements. Without this, the "co-pilot" framing is misleading—the system has never been used by anyone other than its designer.

## 6. Safer Contribution Wording

> We present IGRE (Insight-Gated Research Evolution), a modular architecture for inserting structured human scientific judgment into automated research pipelines at five explicit decision points: hypothesis taste priors, evaluator stress tests, frontier steering, verifiable micro-evolution escalation, and claim calibration. Our primary contribution is a formal gate schema and audit protocol that records human decisions as reproducible, structured artifacts linked to downstream experimental outcomes, enabling honest post-hoc analysis of when and whether human intervention changes the research trajectory. We provide a pilot implementation built on AI Scientist-v2 and OpenEvolve, a rerunnable gate-chain trace, and a prospective matched-budget package validator. Across four prospective packages on FML-bench and a controlled combinatorial task, we find that human-gated branches do not improve average short-budget benchmark performance over autonomous baselines—a negative result that motivates the paper's central design question: which gate types, feedback formats, and budget schedules are necessary conditions for human scientific taste to add value, and how should future systems measure this? We release the gate schema, audit scripts, and single-author trace corpus as infrastructure for the broader automated-science community to build on.
